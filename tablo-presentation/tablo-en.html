Body

On the wall or on the desk

A flat 185 × 146 × 15 mm printed body hangs on two keyholes like a picture frame. For the desk, a slim leg clips into the same keyholes and holds it at 15°, hidden behind the screen. USB-C and the power switch sit on the right edge.

Buttons

Eight buttons that know what they do

A row of low-profile mechanical switches under the screen. Each key sits exactly under its icon, 20.4 mm apart across the full width of the display.

Icons on the screenWhat each key does is set in Home Assistant. Reassign it and the icon updates on its own.
See what's onA filled tile means that light is on. It redraws in under a second after a press, without flashing the screen.
Pills you can feelKeycaps are 3D-printed to match the case. "All off" has a dimple so you can find it without looking.
4

Weather

The next eight hours in one line

Each hour gets an icon and a temperature. Rain strength shows as one, two or three strokes under the cloud, with no percentages or charts. Data comes from Open-Meteo, free and keyless.

Only when it mattersRain is shown only when it's actually likely, so a hesitant forecast doesn't look like a downpour.
One styleThe large icon in the header and the small hourly ones share the same line and sit on the same baseline.
Easy to extendSnow, storms, fog: the service maps the forecast's weather code to the right icon.
5

How it works

Smart server, simple screen

All the heavy lifting happens in a small service on the home server next to Home Assistant: it collects the data, draws a ready-made frame and handles button presses. The device just shows the picture and reports its keys.
