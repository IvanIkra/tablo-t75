# Tablo — software: emulator and firmware (§9–10)

> Part of the software design, index in [`docs/README.md`](../README.md). Section numbers §N run through all the parts.

## 9. Emulator

The page `http://<service>:8765/emu` repeats the firmware logic in JavaScript.

- It connects over **the same protocol** as the board. The service cannot tell it from the hardware.
- An 800×480 canvas, 1× or 2× scale, "paper" colours.
- It draws the clock, the date and the status line itself (with the infomaster icon on `data: false`) by the zones from `config`, in the time zone from `config`. The time and the date use the same pixel fonts as the service (`/fonts/pixel/clock-64.txt` and `Inter-Medium-16.txt`, §6.3). If the fonts fail to load, it draws with plain Inter.
- **Buttons:** 8 buttons under the screen. A click is a short press, holding ≥ 600 ms is a long one, keys `1`–`8`. Instant tile inversion, rollback on `button_result`, full-screen screens with `ttl`, its own diagnostics on button 8.
- **Debugging:** highlighting of the partial-refresh rectangles, a "service unreachable" switch (drops the connection), a log of protocol messages.
- It runs in a browser on the dev machine and takes no server memory.

## 10. Firmware

The code is in `firmware/` (PlatformIO + Arduino), the screen test in `firmware/bringup/`. This section covers what matters for the protocol and the build.

- Libraries: `GxEPD2` (the `GxEPD2_750_GDEY075T7` driver directly, without the `GxEPD2_BW` buffer), `arduinoWebSockets`, `ArduinoJson` 7. `arduinoWebSockets` has a hard 15 KB message limit without an `#ifndef`; the `tools/patch_websockets.py` script raises it to 64 KB before the build (a frame is a single 48 020-byte message) and hands the incoming message buffer to the firmware (`ws_payload_alloc`/`ws_payload_free` in `link.cpp`). `WEBSOCKETS_TCP_TIMEOUT` is 15 s: on bad Wi-Fi a frame takes longer than the default 5 s.
- The flash layout is `min_spiffs.csv`: two 1.9 MB app partitions for OTA. Wi-Fi + WebSocket + OTA do not fit in the standard 1.25 MB.
- The screen is wired to the ESP32-L through the DESPI-C02: CS 27, DC 14, RST 26 (the wire from RES of the screen connector P5 is moved to A19, the RES pin on P5 is empty), BUSY 13, SCK 18, MOSI 23; a 2 ms reset pulse. RST cannot be on GPIO12: it is a strapping pin, the panel holds it high during reset, the ESP32 picks 1.8 V for the flash and falls into a "flash read err" loop. USB uploads are reliable at 115 200 and fail at 921 600.
- **Live panel (30.10.2026):** a full refresh is 2.3 s (the panel itself 1.55 s; since 31.10 the full refresh on button 8, at night, on an HA command, at startup and after `max_partials` partial ones goes through a cycle: the whole screen black, then white, then the frame, about 5.3 s; without it, black areas kept traces. A big change of picture (diagnostics, an overlay) is a plain full refresh, without the cycle), a partial refresh of a 240×120 window is 0.44 s, of the whole screen 0.62 s; the difference is sending 48 KB over SPI at GxEPD2's default speed, which can be raised. The picture is sharp, 1 px edges and lines are visible; after a series of partial refreshes there are faint traces of the past.
- Memory: two 48 KB frame buffers (one holds the base frame, the other receives the next; a valid base frame simply swaps them, with no copy) + the 48 KB shown on screen (the new frame is compared with it). The buffers are taken at startup (one static; two do not fit in `.bss`): the library used to take 48 KB from the heap for the time of receiving, and on bad Wi-Fi the TCP stack fragmented the heap with frame pieces out of order, no block was found and the link dropped (code 1011).
- **The shown image lives in IRAM** (since fw-0.4): 408 of 480 rows in plain IRAM (below `0x400A0000`, the largest block ~40 KB), the other 72 in the ordinary heap. IRAM can only be read and written in 32-bit words, so rows are compared word by word, and the picture goes to the panel driver (which reads bytes) in 16-row bands through a 1.6 KB buffer. Without this the ordinary heap had 18 KB after startup and a few KB once connected: `config` failed to parse (`NoMemory`), connections and receiving broke, the board stopped reconnecting until a restart, and OTA broke off with a restart. `ESP.getFreeHeap()` did not show this: it also counts IRAM that `malloc` does not use (it showed ~52 KB). Now the logs, the diagnostics screen and `status.heap` show the `MALLOC_CAP_8BIT` heap: ~60 KB after startup, ~48 KB once connected. The diagnostics line buffer is also taken at startup: a `std::vector` that runs out of memory restarts the board. A new frame is assembled row by row: a row of the base frame, the firmware zones over it, a comparison with the row on screen. A partial refresh covers the rectangle of changed bytes; a big change is a full refresh (§3.4). After 5 s without updates the panel power is switched off.
- Time comes from NTP, the zone from `config` (`MSK-3`). Until the time is known, the clock zone is empty. Fonts and icons are the same pixel files as the service's (`clock-64`, `Inter-Medium-16` for the date, `Inter-SemiBold-16` for the offline badge, `Inter-SemiBold-30` for diagnostics) and 17 px MDI icons, converted to C tables: `service/.venv/bin/python firmware/tools/gen_assets.py` → `src/assets.{h,cpp}`. The clock digits have one width: `HH:MM` is always 174 px.
- **Buttons 1–8 left to right: GPIO 0, 4, 19, 33, 32, 25, 22, 21** (on the board T1, T0, MISO, A5, A4, A18, SCL, SDA), each between the pin and GND, internal pull-up, no external resistors. 0 is the BOOT button, with the R8 pull-up on the board: holding button 1 at power-on puts the board into flashing mode. That is also the emergency entrance: if broken firmware will not take a new upload, hold button 1, power up and upload over USB. 19 is MISO of the bus that carries the unused font chip (CS GPIO15) and SPI flash (CS GPIO2): the firmware holds both CS high and starts SPI without MISO. The board LED is on 4 — it lights while the button is pressed. **GPIO12 (T5) is not used:** on this board, after the RES rework, it is shorted to GPIO14 (the screen's DC) — pressing button 1 on it turned screen data into commands and hung the panel (31.10.2026, tested with firmware: each pulls the other to ground). The firmware does not configure it, and it does not affect boot. 34–39 do not work without an external pull-up, 5/16/17 are not broken out on the board. Jumper P3 (PWR_Jump) powers the screen; remove it only with a bridge in its place.
- The buttons are not polled in the main loop: GxEPD2 blocks it during a refresh (up to 2.3 s for a full one). Polling is a system timer (`esp_timer`) every 5 ms (20 ms debounce; not a task of its own — its stack would come from the same heap), presses go into a queue with their time; the duration is counted by that time, so short/long is right even if the loop handles the press later. A press is drawn first (the tile is inverted), then sent to the service.
- **The network is a task of its own on core 0** (`link.cpp`): connecting, receiving and sending on bad Wi-Fi take several seconds, and the screen, the clock and the buttons do not wait for that. Everything that touches the panel and the state still runs in the main loop: a received message is handed to it in place (the library buffer) and the network task waits until it is parsed; outgoing ones go through a queue. Stacks: network 7 KB (~4.5 used), main loop 6 KB (~2.2). Tested on the board on 31.10.2026. While the 3D printer prints, frames come every ~30 s (the printer row in Home), so collisions will be frequent — decided to leave it so.
- **The firmware version** is `fw-<number>+<commit>`, e.g. `fw-0.5+aa849f6`: the `FW_NUMBER` in `src/state.h` is raised by hand for a notable change, with `FW_NOTES` next to it — what changed, 1–2 lines in English; the short commit hash is added at build time by `tools/fw_version.py`, with `-dirty` if `firmware/` has uncommitted changes. The version shows on the diagnostics screen, in the startup log and in `hello` to the service; `FW_NOTES` goes in `hello` too, and the service shows it in the greeting after a new version (§5). History: 0.2 — the black-white cycle on a full refresh; 0.3 — `ttl_s = 0` and `close_overlay` for sticky messages; 0.4 — the shown image in IRAM, an honest heap; 0.5 — OTA screens; 0.6 — the dark theme; 0.7 — updates through the service.
- Wi-Fi, the service address, the token and the OTA password are in `include/secrets.h` (the template is `secrets.example.h`; the file itself is not in git). The board's USB-C is brought outside, so it can be flashed without taking the case apart.
- **Flashing over USB:** `pio run -e esp32 -t upload --upload-port <serial-port>`, about 2 min.
- **Over the air through the service** (since fw-0.7, the main path): `pio run -e service -t upload`. `tools/ota_service.py` puts the image to `PUT /api/firmware` (`TABLO_API_TOKEN` and the service address `TABLO_SERVICE` come from the environment or `service/.env`), the service sends the board `ota`, the board downloads `/firmware.bin` in one TCP stream into a free frame buffer, writes it to flash, checks the MD5 and restarts. The script waits for the board to come back with the version from the image (up to 4 min) and says so if it came back with the old one. No byte for 30 s — "Update failed", a restart after 10 s. The GET is done without `HTTPClient`: it added 3 KB of code in IRAM, IRAM then fit 40 fewer screen rows, and the heap while connected dropped from ~48 to ~42 KB. Tested on 31.10: two out of two, 1.4 MB in 100–120 s (the board's Wi-Fi at −65…−69 dBm, slow).
- **Over the air from the dev machine** (espota, the fallback when there is no link to the service): `pio run -e ota -t upload --upload-port <board-ip>` with `TABLO_OTA_PASSWORD` in the environment (`tablo.local` over mDNS works too). ArduinoOTA waits 10 s for each piece instead of the default 1: at 1 s the upload broke off (`error 3`) on a Wi-Fi stall. espota sends 1 KB at a time and waits for an answer to each, also for no more than 10 s (hard-coded in `espota.py`). Failures on fw-0.5–0.6 (one upload in three, on 31.10 one in five): (1) the board drew the update screen before connecting back, and after `max_partials` that was a full refresh taking 5.5 s of the 10 — since fw-0.7 that screen is always partial; (2) on bad Wi-Fi the transfer stalls: pauses of 2–5 s in every upload, sometimes over 10 s, and espota breaks off (`Error Uploading: timed out`). The second cannot be fixed, so the main path goes through the service.
- **Board log:** the serial port at 115 200. Open it with DTR and RTS off (pyserial: `dtr`/`rts = False` before `open()`), or the board restarts; an RTS pulse is a deliberate restart. While the port is open, USB flashing is not possible. To debug the WebSocket library and core errors, build with `PLATFORMIO_BUILD_FLAGS="-DCORE_DEBUG_LEVEL=1 -DDEBUG_ESP_PORT=Serial"`.
- **The board token** is its own since 31.10: the service no longer accepts the default token from the code (401). It is `TABLO_DEVICE_TOKEN` in the service's `.env` on the server and on the dev machine, and the same value in `secrets.h`. Open the emulator as `/emu?token=…`. The API token (for HA) is its own since 18.10.
